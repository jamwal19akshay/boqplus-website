This guide explains how a seller takes part in a GeM bid, from registration to the reverse auction. It uses GeM's published rules as of 7 November 2026. The bid document for each tender has the final say.

## The GeM bidding process, step by step

The steps below follow the order in which a seller meets them. Each one comes from GeM's General Terms and Conditions.

### Register as a seller

Only a Director, Partner or Proprietor (as applicable) can be the Primary User who registers a business as a seller. GeM checks the details you enter against other databases, such as PAN, MCA 21 and Udyam Registration. If the details do not verify, registration is not allowed. Have these records ready before you start.

### List your product or service

You join an e-bid by offering a product or service that is already listed on GeM. GeM normally needs 48 hours to approve or reject an offer before it is listed. GeM tells sellers to list their offer well before the bid closes, so do not leave listing to the last day.

### Watch for the bid invitation

The bid invitation goes to registered sellers who have goods or services listed in that category. A bid or reverse auction notice must be published with at least 10 clear days before the last date for bid submission. Work back from the closing date when you plan your work.

### Keep price out of the technical bid

Put price elements only in the financial bid. Mentioning price in the technical bid can lead to the offer being rejected as non-responsive. The one exception is single-packet bidding.

### Bid security (EMD)

GeM recommends bid security (EMD) of 1% of the estimated value of procurement. The buyer can choose between 0.5% and 5%. The bid security should stay valid for 45 days beyond the bid validity. There is no bid security for bids or reverse auctions with estimated value below Rs 5 lakh. For how deposits are refunded or forfeited, read [Tender EMD Explained](/blog/tender-emd-earnest-money-deposit).

### Offer validity

A seller's offer in an e-bid stays valid for 15 days from the bid opening date, or as the buyer states in the bid document. For a Bid to RA, the offer stays valid for 30 days from the date the reverse auction starts.

### If the bid is a reverse auction

The start or reference price and the step value of decrement are shown to bidders at the start. A bidder can bid one or more step decrements below the prevailing lowest bid. If a lower bid arrives within the last 15 minutes, the end time extends automatically by another 15 minutes.

## Planning your timing

Three time limits matter most. The notice must be published at least 10 clear days before the last date for bid submission. Your listing may need GeM's approval time before you can offer. Your offer then has to stay valid for the period the bid document sets, and your bid security should cover the validity plus the extra days GeM mentions. Put these dates together before you commit to a bid.

## What a seller can do with this

- Make sure the person registering your business is a Director, Partner or Proprietor, as applicable. Only they can be the Primary User.
- Check that your registration details match your PAN, MCA 21 and Udyam records before you register.
- Keep a list of the categories where your products or services are listed. Bid invitations go to registered sellers with listings in that category.
- Work back from the last date for bid submission. Allow time for listing approval, which normally needs 48 hours.
- Read the validity and EMD clauses in each bid document before you set your own price and timing.
- Keep price figures out of the technical part of your bid.
- For background on how tender results are read, see [How to Read a Comparative Statement in a Tender](/blog/how-to-read-tender-comparative-statement) and [L1 Tender Meaning](/blog/what-is-l1-in-tender).

## Caveats to keep in mind

Rules differ between buyers. The GeM General Terms and Conditions apply unless they are superseded by category-specific Special Terms and Conditions or bid-specific Additional Terms and Conditions. The bid document is final.

State government buyers can set some conditions differently. One example is whether to ask for EMD from MSEs.

This guide reads version 1.29 of the GeM General Terms and Conditions, dated 25 March 2026. GeM revises these terms from time to time, so check the current version on the GeM site before you bid.

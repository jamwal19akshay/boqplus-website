This article explains how the CPWD Delhi Schedule of Rates (DSR) and state schedules of rates are used when a department prepares an estimate, and what to check in your tender. The information is as of 21 November 2026.

## CPWD DSR vs State SOR: Which Schedule of Rates Applies

The short answer is that it depends on the department and the tender. The tender document tells you which schedule was used. The sections below explain the two main kinds of schedule and how they reach your BOQ.

### What a schedule of rates is

A schedule of rates is a department's official list of item descriptions and rates. The department uses it to prepare estimates.

### Where the CPWD DSR is used

The CPWD Delhi Schedule of Rates is published by the Central Public Works Department. It is used for central works. Many other departments also use it as a reference.

To see what the book covers, read [what the CPWD DSR holds](/blog/cpwd-dsr-2023-explained).

### How state schedules of rates fit in

Most states publish their own schedule of rates. It may be called SOR or SSR. It is usually published through the state's public works department. State works are estimated from it.

So on a state job, the estimate is likely to be built from the state's own book rather than the CPWD DSR. Do not assume which one applies. Read your tender.

### Which schedule applies to your tender

The tender document states which schedule and which cost index were used. Read it before you start pricing. If it is not clear to you, ask the department.

## How a Schedule Feeds a Percentage-Rate Tender

In a percentage-rate tender, the estimate is built from the schedule's rates. The contractor's percentage is then quoted against those rates.

That is why the schedule and its edition matter. Your percentage is applied to rates taken from that book, so you need to know exactly which book it is.

If you are not sure how this differs from entering a rate for each item, read [item rate vs percentage rate tender](/blog/item-rate-vs-percentage-rate-tender).

## Revisions and Cost Indexes

Schedules are revised from time to time. Departments may also apply a cost index or escalation to an older schedule. The tender document states which schedule and which cost index were used.

For more on how an index relates to schedule rates, read [CPWD cost index explained](/blog/cpwd-cost-index-explained).

## What You Can Do With This

Here is a simple routine to follow when a new tender arrives:

1. Open the tender document and find the schedule it names.
2. Note the edition of that schedule.
3. Note whether a cost index or escalation is named.
4. Check that the item descriptions in the BOQ match the schedule you are using.
5. Compare your own material and labour quotes with the schedule's rate for the same item.

A few more points:

- The schedule is a reference. It is not your cost. Your own quotes and site conditions still need to be checked.
- To understand what sits inside a unit rate, read [rate analysis of civil works](/blog/rate-analysis-civil-works-basics).
- BOQ+ includes CPWD DSR and state rate books. The [sources page](/sources) lists the editions it uses, so compare them with the edition named in your tender.

## Caveats

Check the edition and cost index named in your tender. Schedules are revised, and a department may apply a cost index or escalation to an older schedule. A schedule is a department's list of rates for preparing estimates. It does not tell you what your own work will cost.

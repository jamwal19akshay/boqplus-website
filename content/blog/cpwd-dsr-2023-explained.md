This guide explains what CPWD DSR 2023 contains, how the Delhi Analysis of Rates shows the build-up of each item, and what the CPWD Works Manual 2026 says about estimates when specifications change. The details are as of 10 November 2026.

## What CPWD DSR 2023 covers

The Delhi Schedule of Rates (DSR) 2023 is issued by CPWD in two civil volumes, Vol I and Vol II. CPWD circulars refer to both volumes and to numbered correction slips for each.

Electrical and mechanical items are in a separate book. CPWD's circular list refers to the Delhi Schedule of Rates (E and M) 2025, with a correction slip dated 27.11.2025, and earlier to the E&M 2018 edition. Check which book a tender names before you price from it.

CPWD's Works Manual 2026 says the Schedule of Rates for Delhi is issued by DG, CPWD, and that SORs are ordinarily revised once in two years.

### Correction slips change the book

DSR 2023 has been updated by correction slips since November 2023. The latest on CPWD's list is Correction Slip No 12 of DSR 2023 for Reinforced Cement Concrete Works, dated 04.08.2026. Look for slips that affect your items before you price them.

### A revision circular

A CPWD circular dated 25.09.2026 is titled 'Revision of Delhi Schedule of Rates and Delhi Analysis of Rates 2023'. Check CPWD's website for what it changes before you rely on the 2023 books.

## How the Delhi Analysis of Rates builds up an item

The Delhi Analysis of Rates (DAR) 2023 also comes in two volumes. It shows how the rate of each item is built up from materials, labour and other components. The DAR foreword calls it the basis for the rates of items in the DSR.

The DAR 2023 preface says it is based on market rates of materials prevailing during April 2023 at Delhi. Labour rates are the minimum wages in force from 01.04.2023. It is the revised edition of CPWD DAR 2021.

In the item analyses, CPWD adds the following in this order:

1. 1% water charges, added to the cost of materials, labour and machinery.
2. GST. The preface says GST at 18% on work contracts is included in the analysis of all items, and basic material rates are taken without GST.
3. 15% contractor's profit and overheads, shown as CPOH.
4. 1% cess.

This is how CPWD builds up its own rates. Your own costs will differ.

## Why the build-up matters for your tender

DSR item rates are based on particular specifications. The CPWD Works Manual 2026 says that if the specifications change while the NIT is being framed, the rates of the corresponding items are to be modified and the estimated cost put to tender corrected. A change in specification can change the estimate, not only the rate.

For how item-rate and percentage-rate tenders differ, see [item rate vs percentage rate tender](/blog/item-rate-vs-percentage-rate-tender). For one item looked at against DSR 2023, see the [centering and shuttering rate article](/blog/shuttering-rate-per-sqm-lintels-beams-dsr-2023).

## What a contractor can do with this

- Confirm the volume, part and edition named in the tender document.
- Check CPWD's circular lists for correction slips on each item you price.
- Read the DAR build-up for your item, so you can see which parts are materials, labour and add-ons.
- Note the price basis: Delhi, April 2023. Your location, lead, carriage, site conditions and price changes since then all affect your own cost. Test the build-up against your own material quotes and labour rates.
- Compare the item's specification with the one in the tender. The Works Manual says rates change when specifications change.

## Caveats to keep in mind

GST rates and other inputs may have changed since April 2023, and CPWD correction slips apply. The tender document and the department's estimate are final.

State PWDs publish their own schedules of rates. The DSR is Delhi-based, so a state tender may use a different book.

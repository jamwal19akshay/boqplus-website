This guide explains what the BidIQ report in BOQ+ shows, how to read it, and what it does not claim. The information is as of 17 November 2026.

## Reading a BidIQ report in the BOQ app

BidIQ is a tab in the app. Its Intelligence view lets you choose one of your priced BOQs and read how it sits against past tender results. If you have not installed the app yet, see [BOQ App Download: How to Install BOQ+ and Sign In](/blog/boq-plus-app-download-and-sign-in).

The numbers come from comparative statements. These are the sheets departments publish after tender opening. They list every bidder and every amount. To see what those sheets contain, read [How to Read a Comparative Statement in a Tender](/blog/how-to-read-tender-comparative-statement).

### Item-rate BOQs

An item is compared only when at least 3 similar past tenders back it. If an item has less evidence than that, BidIQ does not compare it.

The report then builds a total range by adding each compared item’s own typical range. It says whether your total is below, within or above that range.

If too few items have enough evidence, BidIQ says so and does not show a total range. In that case the app gives you no total to compare against.

### Percentage tenders

For a percentage tender, BidIQ shows the range of winning percentages on similar past tenders, below or above the estimate. It needs at least 3 similar tenders. It shows a “typical” figure only when there are 20 or more.

If you are unsure how percentage tenders differ from item-rate tenders, read [Difference Between Item Rate and Percentage Rate Tender](/blog/item-rate-vs-percentage-rate-tender).

### What the thresholds mean

The thresholds tell you how much past evidence sits behind a figure.

- For an item-rate BOQ, an item needs at least 3 similar past tenders before it is compared.
- For a percentage tender, a range needs at least 3 similar tenders.
- A “typical” figure for a percentage tender appears only with 20 or more similar tenders. With fewer, you see a range but no typical figure.

So a range built on a few tenders and a range built on many are not the same kind of evidence. Note how much data sits behind what you are reading.

### The two cards to check

A card called “What is moving your bid” lists the lines where your rate sits furthest from the typical market rate. A separate card lists high-value lines priced without enough comparable evidence.

The first card shows where your rates sit furthest from the typical market rate. The second shows where the evidence is thin on high-value lines.

### Who has bid here before

This section shows historical appearances only. The app states that nothing in it says who will bid this time. Do not read it as a list of likely rivals.

## What the report does not claim

BidIQ does not predict the result. The app says: “Historical comparison is not a prediction of this tender.”

The PDF report carries a disclaimer in the same spirit. It says that past tender evidence is no assurance of how future tenders turn out, and that final bid decisions remain with the contractor. BidIQ’s wording is factual. It does not use recommendation language.

The professional PDF report is included with a Quarterly or Annual plan. Otherwise, it uses 1 credit.

## What a contractor can do with the report

1. Price your BOQ first. The steps are in [How to Price a BOQ in BOQ+](/blog/price-e-tender-boq-file-in-boq-plus).
2. Open the BidIQ tab, go to the Intelligence view and choose the priced BOQ. Read the total range, if the app shows one.
3. If no total range is shown, note what the app says. The data for that BOQ may be too thin to compare.
4. Read the “What is moving your bid” card. Check those lines against your own material quotes, labour rates, lead and wastage.
5. Read the card for high-value lines priced without enough comparable evidence. For those lines, your own quotes and costs are the main evidence you have.
6. For a percentage tender, note how many similar tenders sit behind the range. Read the range as past results only.
7. Keep a copy of the report with your bid file. The final decision stays with you.

For where the underlying data comes from, see [Rate books and data sources BOQ+ uses](/sources). Common questions are answered on the [BOQ+ FAQ](/faq).

## Caveats to keep in mind

- Screens and button names may change between app versions.
- BidIQ shows past tender results. It does not promise a win, a margin or a saving.
- Where the data is thin for a department or item, the app says so instead of showing a number.
- Report wording and layout are produced by the app and server and can change.

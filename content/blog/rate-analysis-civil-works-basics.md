This guide explains what goes into a unit rate for civil work, as of 12 November 2026. You will see how rate analysis builds the cost of one unit of an item, and how to use that when you price a BOQ.

## What rate analysis of civil works covers

Rate analysis works out the cost of one unit of an item of work. Take one cubic metre of concrete as an example. The rate for that unit is built from its inputs, one by one. This lets you see what sits behind a rate before you enter it in a BOQ.

A rate is built from a few main parts:

- Materials, worked out as quantity per unit, with wastage included.
- Labour, worked out as man-days per unit.
- Machinery and carriage.
- Water charges, where they apply.

Contractor's overheads and profit are added on top of that cost.

## How the build-up works

A rate is built up from these parts:

1. Start with the item and its unit, such as one cubic metre.
2. Work out how much of each material the unit needs, including wastage.
3. Work out the labour needed for one unit, in man-days.
4. Add machinery and carriage for the item.
5. Add water charges where the work needs them.
6. Add contractor's overheads and profit on top of that cost.
7. Handle taxes as the tender document specifies.

Do not assume every BOQ treats tax the same way. Taxes such as GST are handled as the tender document specifies, and some BOQs ask for rates inclusive of taxes. Read the tender document before you enter any figure.

### Why the rate changes with location

Rates change with location. Carriage distance, local material prices and labour rates differ between sites. A rate built for one site may not match the cost at another.

Schedules of rates show how their own rates were built in their rate analysis. Reading that build-up shows you the inputs behind a schedule rate. You can then ask which of those inputs are different on your site.

## Practical steps for your own pricing

Use a rate analysis as a checklist, not as something to copy. A contractor can do the following:

- Pick one item from your BOQ and write down each input: materials, labour, machinery, carriage and water.
- Replace generic inputs with your own material quotes and your own labour rates.
- Compare your build-up with the schedule rate for the same item, and note where they differ.
- Compare your rate with what other bidders quoted for the same item, where those results are published. This can help you spot items that look priced too high or too low.
- Check how the tender treats taxes before you settle any rate.

Comparing rates is a check on your own working. It does not tell you what figure to enter. ## Where to check the detail

This guide is an outline. Departments publish detailed rate analysis alongside their schedules of rates, and that is the document to check for any item. The page on [rate books and data sources BOQ+ uses](/sources) lists those sources.

## Caveats

This is an outline; departments publish detailed analysis with their schedules. The steps above describe the general structure of a rate only. They do not give a rate for any item, and they do not say what any bid should be. Carriage distance, local material prices and labour rates differ between sites, so a rate from one site or department may not fit another. Check each item against your own costs.

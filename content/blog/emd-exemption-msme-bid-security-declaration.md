This guide explains when Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs) can skip earnest money deposit (EMD) in a tender, and what a Bid Security Declaration asks of you instead. The rules below are as of 13 November 2026.

People often search for "MSME", but the rules and policies quoted here name Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs), so that is the term used throughout.

## EMD Exemption for MSME Bidders: What the Rules Say

### What the Rule Says

Under GFR 2017 Rule 170(i), as revised by a Department of Expenditure OM dated 25 July 2017, bid security (also called Earnest Money) is to be obtained in advertised or limited tender enquiries. The exceptions are Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs) as defined in the MSE Procurement Policy, firms registered with the Central Purchase Organisation or the concerned Ministry or Department, and Startups recognised by DIPP.

Where bid security is taken, the amount should ordinarily range between two percent and five percent of the estimated value of the goods to be procured. It is normally to remain valid for forty-five days beyond the final bid validity period. The rule's wording refers to goods, so check how your tender treats works.

The rule lists the forms bid security may take. Among them are Account Payee Demand Draft, Fixed Deposit Receipt, Banker's Cheque, and online payment in an acceptable form. The Manual for Procurement of Works (June 2022) also lists Insurance Surety Bonds.

### The Bid Security Declaration

A Department of Expenditure OM dated 12 November 2020 (F.9/4/2020-PPD) noted that MSEs and firms registered with the concerned Ministries or Departments are exempted from submitting bid security under Rule 170. In lieu of bid security, bidders may be asked to sign a Bid Security Declaration.

In a declaration, the bidder accepts that if it withdraws or modifies its bid during the validity period, it will be suspended for the time specified in the tender documents. So the declaration is a commitment about your conduct while the bid is valid. Read the suspension period before you sign.

The same OM said that future bid documents should carry no provisions for bid security, only a provision for a Bid Security Declaration. It said the instructions applied to tenders issued till 31.12.2021.

The OM also covers the case where there are compelling circumstances to ask for bid security. That should be done only with the approval of the next higher authority to the one competent to finalise the tender, or the Secretary of the Ministry or Department, whichever is lower.

### Works Tenders Need a Closer Look

The MSE Public Procurement Policy Order 2012 lists exemption from earnest money among its benefits. But the Ministry's FAQs say the Policy covers goods produced and services rendered by MSEs, and that works contracts are excluded from it. The FAQs also say the Policy does not exempt MSEs from security deposit or performance bank security.

CPWD's Works Manual 2026 (para 5.1.1) says earnest money is obtained from bidders for all tenders unless otherwise mentioned in the tender documents. The text of the manual that was read does not list an MSE exemption for CPWD works tenders.

### GeM Tenders

On GeM, Micro and Small Enterprises holding a valid Udyam Registration who are the manufacturer of the offered product or service are among the categories exempt from furnishing bid security. DPIIT-recognised startups with a valid certificate are also exempt.

Buyers cannot take EMD from an exempt category of seller through bid-specific conditions. State government buyers and bodies may choose to seek EMD from MSE bidders by saying so in the bid conditions. For the wider process, see [how a seller takes part in a GeM bid](/blog/gem-bidding-process-for-sellers).

## What a Contractor Can Do With This

1. Read the EMD clause in the tender document first. Check whether it asks for EMD, accepts a Bid Security Declaration, or accepts MSE or startup certificates.
2. Keep your Udyam Registration and any other certificate the tender names ready to upload or show.
3. If you are asked to sign a declaration, note the suspension period it sets and the bid validity period it applies to.
4. Plan for the security deposit and performance security separately. The FAQs say the MSE Policy does not exempt MSEs from them.
5. If the tender asks for EMD, read [our guide on tender EMD](/blog/tender-emd-earnest-money-deposit) for how deposits, refunds and forfeiture work.
6. To see what a tender expects in each part of your submission, read [technical bid and financial bid](/blog/technical-bid-vs-financial-bid).

## Caveats to Keep in Mind

Rules differ between departments and states. The tender document is final, so check it for every bid: whether it asks for EMD, accepts a Bid Security Declaration, or accepts MSE or startup certificates.

The 12 November 2020 OM applied to tenders issued till 31.12.2021. Check the tender document to see whether a Bid Security Declaration is accepted now.

For reference, typical EMD under GFR is 2% to 5%. CPWD works use 2% of estimated cost up to Rs ten crore. GeM buyers choose 0.5% to 5% (1% recommended). These are general figures, not the terms of any particular tender.

This guide explains what L1, L2 and L3 mean in a tender and how the lowest bidder is decided. The information is as of 1 November 2026.

## L1 tender meaning: what L1, L2 and L3 mean

L1 means the lowest bidder. It is the bid with the lowest evaluated price among the technically qualified bids.

The other ranks follow the same logic:

- **L1:** the lowest bid among the technically qualified bids.
- **L2:** the second lowest.
- **L3:** the third lowest.
- And so on down the list.

The "L" stands for lowest. A bidder who is L2 is simply next in price order after L1.

## How the lowest bidder is decided

The order of events matters. Technical evaluation comes first. Only bids that qualify technically are ranked.

The ranking is then made when the financial bids are opened. That is the point at which the prices are compared and the L1, L2 and L3 positions are known.

The ranking is on the *evaluated* price. How a department evaluates a price is set by that department, so read the tender document for the details of your tender.

### Percentage-rate tenders

Not every tender asks you to quote item rates. In a percentage-rate tender, bidders quote a percentage against the estimate.

The ranking follows the total amount after each bidder's percentage is applied to the estimate. So the totals are what get compared.

### When two bidders quote the same

Ties at L1 happen when two or more bidders quote exactly the same. The department then follows its own tie-breaking rule, such as a draw of lots. The rule differs by department, so check what your department says.

## Why L1 does not always get the work

Being L1 does not by itself mean the work is awarded. Under its rules, the department can:

- negotiate,
- re-tender, or
- reject bids.

The department can also check whether a very low rate is workable.

So L1 is a ranking, not a final decision. It tells you the order of the prices. It does not tell you what the department will do next. The tender document and the department's rules are where to look for that.

## Where to find the L1, L2 and L3 ranking

Many e-procurement portals publish the bid opening summary. Many also publish a comparative statement. This shows every qualified bidder's price and their L1, L2 ranking.

Not every portal does this, and the sheet we work from does not say which ones do. If a portal publishes neither document for your tender, you will not find the ranking there.

## What a contractor can do with this information

1. Find the tender on the portal and note the bid opening date.
2. After the financial bids are opened, look for the bid opening summary and the comparative statement, if the portal publishes them.
3. Note the L1, L2 and L3 prices as shown in the statement.
4. In a percentage-rate tender, remember that the ranking follows the total amount after the percentage is applied to the estimate.
5. Read the tender clauses on negotiation, re-tender and rejection before drawing conclusions from the ranking.
6. Compare the published prices with your own BOQ rates, so you can see how your pricing sits against the bids that were opened.

## Caveats and limits

Award rules vary by department. This is a general explanation, not legal advice.

The ranking shows the order of prices among technically qualified bids. It does not show whether a bid will be accepted, negotiated, rejected or sent for re-tender. Tie-breaking and price checks also follow each department's own rules, so always read the tender document for your tender.

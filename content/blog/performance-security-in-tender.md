This guide explains what performance security is in a works contract, how much is usually asked, which forms it can take and when it is returned. The figures below are as of 19 November 2026.

## Performance security in tender: what it is in a works contract

Performance security is the security a contractor furnishes to the department after the work is awarded. The Manual for Procurement of Works (June 2022) says performance security is forfeited if the contractor breaches the contract.

For the deposit that goes with a bid, see [Tender EMD explained](/blog/tender-emd-earnest-money-deposit).

## How much performance security is asked

The Manual says performance security should be five to ten per cent of the value of the contract. In works contracts it is usual to take five percent.

The percentage depends on the department and the tender. Some examples:

- CPWD Works Manual 2026 (para 5.2) sets it at 5% of the Estimated Cost put to Tender or the contract amount, whichever is higher.
- On GeM, the buyer sets the percentage, up to 5% of contract value.

The Manual also says performance security is not necessary for a contract value up to Rs one lakh.

### The 3% relaxation

A Department of Expenditure OM dated 12 November 2020 reduced performance security from the then 5-10% to 3% of the value of the contract. It applied to existing contracts, and to tenders and contracts issued or concluded till 31.12.2021. Contracts under arbitration or court proceedings did not get the benefit.

A later OM dated 30.12.2021 extended the 3% relaxation to 31.03.2023, according to the June 2022 Manual. A contract that took the 3% rate keeps it for the whole contract. For a new tender, check the tender document for the rate that applies now. Do not assume the 3% rate applies.

## Forms performance security can take

The Manual lists these forms:

- Insurance Surety Bond
- Account payee demand draft
- Fixed deposit receipt from a commercial bank
- Bank BG, issued or confirmed by a commercial bank in India
- Online payment in an acceptable form

The tender conditions state which forms are accepted for that work.

## When performance security must be submitted

Under the Manual, performance security is to be furnished by a specified date, generally 21 days after notification of the award. Another part of the Manual says generally 14 days. The tender document states the actual period.

Other rules set their own times:

- CPWD: the time to submit is set in the NIT, but not beyond 7 days of issue of the letter of intent.
- GeM: it must be submitted within 15 days of award.

## When performance security is returned

Under the Manual, performance security should remain valid for 60 days beyond completion of all contractual obligations, including the Defect Liability Period. It is refunded without interest after the contractor completes all obligations. The refund must come no later than 365 days after the end of the Defect Liability Period.

Other rules differ:

- CPWD: valid for at least six months beyond completion of all contractual obligations.
- GeM: valid for 2 months beyond all contractual obligations, and refunded within 30 days of completion.

Separately, works contracts often also withhold a security deposit or retention money from each running bill. The tender conditions set how this works.

## What a contractor can do with this information

Before you price a tender, read the performance security clause and note these points:

1. The percentage, and whether it applies to the contract value or to the estimate.
2. The forms accepted, and whether your bank can issue the one you need.
3. The submission date, counted from the award or letter of intent.
4. Any security deposit or retention money taken from running bills.
5. The validity period, which runs past completion and the Defect Liability Period.

Allow for the cost of arranging the security, and for the time it stays locked up, in your cost and cash-flow plan. If you also bid on GeM, see [GeM bidding process for sellers](/blog/gem-bidding-process-for-sellers) for how a seller takes part in a bid. For bid-stage rules for small firms, see [EMD exemption for MSMEs and bid security declaration](/blog/emd-exemption-msme-bid-security-declaration).

## Caveats

Rules differ between departments and states. CPWD, railways, PSUs and state PWDs each set their own percentage and forms in their tender conditions. The tender document is final.

The 3% relaxation was time-limited. Under the June 2022 Manual it ran up to 31.03.2023, so do not assume it applies to a current tender.

The Manual gives the submission period as generally 21 days in one place and generally 14 days in another. The tender document states the actual period.

According to the Ministry's FAQs, the MSE procurement policy does not exempt MSEs from performance security.

This guide explains the difference between an item rate tender and a percentage rate tender, and how each BOQ is filled. It reflects the position as of 4 November 2026.

## Item rate tender vs percentage rate tender: what is different

The two types differ in who sets the item rates. In an item-rate tender, the contractor quotes a rate for each item in the BOQ. The BOQ sheet multiplies each rate by its quantity and adds the results into the total bid.

In a percentage-rate tender, the department fixes the item rates, usually from its schedule of rates. The contractor does not enter rates for each item. Instead, the contractor quotes one percentage above, below or at par with the estimated cost.

The tender notice (NIT) and the BOQ file state which type a tender uses. Check both before you start, because the way the BOQ is filled is different for each type.

## The two types side by side

- **Who sets the item rates:** in an item-rate tender, you do. In a percentage-rate tender, the department does, usually from its schedule of rates.
- **What you enter:** in an item-rate tender, a rate for each item. In a percentage-rate tender, one percentage, with 'Above', 'Below' or 'At Par'.
- **How the total is formed:** in an item-rate tender, the sheet multiplies each rate by its quantity and adds the amounts. In a percentage-rate tender, your single percentage is set against the estimated cost.

## Where you are likely to see each type

Percentage-rate BOQs are common for civil works on state e-procurement portals. Item-rate BOQs are common where items are not in the schedule of rates, or where the department wants item-wise prices.

So a contractor working on state portals may meet either type. Do not assume the type from the kind of work alone. The NIT and the BOQ file decide it.

## How each BOQ is filled

### Item-rate BOQ

You enter a rate against every item. The sheet then does the arithmetic. Each rate is multiplied by its quantity, and the amounts are added into the total bid. Because every line carries your own rate, each line feeds into the total.

### Percentage-rate BOQ

You do not enter a rate for each item. Usually, you choose 'Above', 'Below' or 'At Par' from a drop-down and then enter the percentage. A wrong choice changes the bid completely, so check it before you submit.

For the wider steps of filling a BOQ, see the [step-by-step guide to filling a BOQ for an e-tender](/blog/how-to-fill-boq-government-e-tender).

## How the lowest bid is decided

The lowest evaluated bid is called L1 in both types. In a percentage tender, it is the bid with the lowest total after applying the percentage. If you are not sure what L1, L2 and L3 mean, read the [L1 tender meaning explainer](/blog/what-is-l1-in-tender).

Quoting below the estimate is common in contested percentage tenders. How far below varies by state, department and type of work.

## What you can do with this information

Use these steps before you price any tender:

1. Read the NIT and the BOQ file to confirm which type the tender uses.
2. Note whether the BOQ asks for item rates or a percentage. Do not assume the type from a past tender.
3. For a percentage tender, check the drop-down choice and the percentage you entered before you submit.
4. For an item-rate tender, check that each rate is in the right line and that the sheet's total matches your own working.
5. Compare your own cost for each item with the department's estimate, so you know how far your cost is from it.

## Caveats

Rules differ between departments. The tender document is final, so follow the NIT and the BOQ file for your tender, even where this guide describes the usual approach.

This guide explains what the CPWD cost index is, who issues it, and how CPWD uses it to bring older rates up to date. Figures in this guide are as of 15 November 2026.

## Cost Index CPWD: The Basics

CPWD's Works Manual 2026 defines a cost index as an indicator of the average cost movement over time of a fixed basket of representative goods and services related to execution of works. In plain words, it tracks how the cost of building work moves over time, using a fixed basket of goods and services.

According to the Works Manual, the cost index is issued as on 1st April and 1st October every year. The cost index for Delhi is issued by CE (CSQ) Civil. The cost index for other places is issued by the ADG or SDG, based on details from the CE or SE concerned.

## How the Base Works

An index is only meaningful with its base. Each Plinth Area Rates (PAR) edition resets the base to 100. CPWD's PAR 2025 says its Plinth Area Rates have base 01.04.2025 as 100.

This matters when you compare figures across editions. PAR 2025's own list shows Delhi at 103 on 01.04.2025 on the older PAR 2023 base, and 100 on 01.04.2025 on the new PAR 2025 base. Both figures are for the same date, but they sit on different bases. Always check the base before you read an index figure.

## Delhi Building Cost Index Figures

Two CPWD orders give Delhi figures on the PAR 2025 base:

- CPWD OM dated 07.01.2026 approved the Delhi Building Cost Index over plinth area rates (PAR) 2025 as 97 as on 01.10.2025.
- CPWD OM dated 27.04.2026 approved the Delhi Building Cost Index over plinth area rates (PAR)-2025 as 101 as on 01.04.2026.

Both refer to PAR-2025 with base 100, effective from 01.04.2025. Each figure describes only the date it is given for.

## How CPWD Uses an Index on Rates

CPWD uses a cost index to bring older rates up to date. For example, PAR 2025 says a few items' rates were arrived at by enhancing their PAR 2023 rates by 103% (the cost index of Delhi as on 01.04.2025).

The index is built from a basket of inputs. The PAR 2025 proforma for the Building Cost Index weights components such as bricks, cement, steel, aggregates, sand, flooring, paints, doors and windows, pipes, lamps and fans, electrical machinery, wires and cables, and labour. For a local index it says only bare rates at the respective station are considered.

If you work from the DSR, [CPWD DSR 2023 Explained](/blog/cpwd-dsr-2023-explained) covers what that schedule holds.

## What a Contractor Can Do With This

1. Check the base and date of every index figure you read. A figure on the PAR 2023 base and a figure on the PAR 2025 base are on different bases.
2. Find out whether the department has enhanced older rates with a cost index. The estimate and the tender document should show what was applied.
3. Note the index date. The Delhi figures above are for 01.10.2025 and 01.04.2026 only.
4. For a location outside Delhi, ask which station index applies and who issued it. Station indices are issued by CPWD's regional offices for their areas.
5. If you build your own rate, see [Rate Analysis of Civil Works](/blog/rate-analysis-civil-works-basics) for the parts that make up a unit rate.

For EPC contracts, the Works Manual 2026 says the reasonability of the L1 tender can be checked using PAR rates duly enhanced by the current cost index or another approved method.

## Caveats to Keep in Mind

- How a department applies a cost index to item rates in its own estimate is set by its own rules. Check the estimate and the tender document.
- Regional (station) indices are issued by CPWD's regional offices for their areas. Delhi figures are Delhi figures; other places have their own station indices.
- Rules and practice differ between CPWD, state PWDs and other departments. The tender document and the department's estimate are final.

This guide explains what EMD means in a tender, how it is paid, and when it is returned or lost. The rules described here are as of 3 November 2026.

## Tender EMD: what bidders need to know

EMD stands for Earnest Money Deposit. It is also called bid security. It is an amount a bidder deposits with its bid to show it is serious about the work.

Each tender sets its own EMD amount and rules in the tender document.

### EMD at a glance

- **What it is:** a deposit made with the bid to show the bidder is serious.
- **Where the terms are:** the tender notice (NIT) states the amount and the accepted forms of payment.
- **Unsuccessful bidders:** the EMD is returned after the tender is decided.
- **Successful bidder:** the EMD is usually adjusted against the performance security, or refunded once it is given.
- **Forfeiture:** the EMD can be forfeited in the cases listed below.

### How EMD is paid

The tender notice, called the NIT, states the EMD amount and the accepted forms of payment. Online payment through the portal is one example. Check the NIT for the exact options before you plan your payment.

On most e-procurement portals, the bid cannot be submitted until the EMD and tender fee steps are completed. So do these well before the deadline, not on the last day.

Some tenders allow exemption from EMD for certain registered firms. The NIT says who qualifies and what proof to upload. If you think you may qualify, read this part of the NIT early so you have the proof ready.

### When EMD is returned or adjusted

The EMD of unsuccessful bidders is returned after the tender is decided.

For the successful bidder, the EMD is usually adjusted against the performance security, or refunded once the performance security is given. The word "usually" matters here: check your own NIT for how it applies to your tender.

### When EMD can be forfeited

EMD can be forfeited in these cases:

1. The bidder withdraws or changes the bid after the deadline.
2. The successful bidder does not sign the agreement or give the performance security on time.

Read the NIT for your tender. Do not rely on memory or on what another tender said.

## What a contractor can do with this

EMD is an early step, but it can hold up a bid if it is left too late. These steps help:

- Find the EMD amount and the accepted payment forms in the NIT as soon as you download it.
- Note the bid deadline on the portal, and complete your EMD payment in the form the NIT accepts well before it.
- If you may qualify for an exemption, check who the NIT says qualifies and prepare the proof to upload.
- Keep a record of your EMD payment, so you can match it later to the refund or adjustment.
- Treat the performance security as a separate step to complete on time, since the successful bidder's EMD is usually linked to it.
- Before you decide to change or withdraw a bid after the deadline, remember that this can lead to forfeiture of your EMD.

## Limits of this guide

### Check each tender

Amounts and rules are set in each tender document. This guide describes how EMD generally works. It does not give an EMD amount for any tender, and it does not cover every case.

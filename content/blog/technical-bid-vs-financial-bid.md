This guide explains what goes into the technical bid and the financial bid in a tender, and when each one is opened. The rules described here are as of 9 November 2026.

## Technical bid and financial bid: how the two envelopes work

In the two-envelope system described in the Manual for Procurement of Works, a bidder splits its offer into two parts. The first part is a techno-commercial bid. The second part is a financial bid. Price and related financial details are left out of the first part.

### What goes in the technical bid

The first envelope holds the eligibility, technical quality and performance aspects of your offer. It also holds the commercial terms and conditions and the documents the tender asks for. Price and related financial details are not in it.

In plain terms, the technical bid answers two questions. Are you eligible? Does your offer meet what the tender asks for? Check each condition in the tender document and match it to a document in your file.

### What goes in the financial bid

The second envelope holds the price quotations along with other financial details. Your rates and amounts go here.

On GeM, price must appear only in the financial bid. Mentioning price in the technical bid can get the offer rejected. Under GeM's 'Bid to RA' option, sellers submit technical and commercial bids before bid closure, and a reverse auction follows the technical evaluation. For the steps a seller follows on that platform, see the [GeM bidding process guide](/blog/gem-bidding-process-for-sellers).

### When each bid is opened

The techno-commercial bids are opened first. They are evaluated against the parameters in the tender document. Only bidders found responsive, eligible and technically compliant go forward.

The financial bids are opened later, on a pre-announced date and time. Only the financial bids of techno-commercially compliant bidders are opened. In e-procurement, the financial bids of technically non-compliant offers do not get opened.

### Two-bid and three-bid systems in CPWD

CPWD's Two Bid System has Bid-1 as the Technical cum eligibility bid and Bid-2 as the Financial bid. Its Three Bid System is used when technical specifications are not final. It has Bid-1 for eligibility, Bid-2 for technical specifications and Bid-3 for the financial bid.

## What a contractor can do with this

- Read the tender document first. It decides what goes in each bid and when the bids are opened.
- Build the technical bid as a checklist. List each eligibility condition and each document the tender asks for, and tick them off before upload.
- Keep price out of the technical bid. Check every form and upload slot before you submit.
- Note the date and time of the financial bid opening from the tender notice.
- If you price the BOQ on your phone, follow the steps in [How to Price a BOQ in BOQ+](/blog/price-e-tender-boq-file-in-boq-plus). For the filling steps on an e-tender, see [How to Fill a BOQ for an E-Tender](/blog/how-to-fill-boq-government-e-tender).
- Once financial bids are opened, see [How to Read a Comparative Statement in a Tender](/blog/how-to-read-tender-comparative-statement). For what the L1, L2 and L3 labels mean, see [L1 Tender Meaning](/blog/what-is-l1-in-tender).

## If your technical bid is rejected

A bidder whose techno-commercial bid is rejected has the right to be heard. It can send a written representation. The procuring entity may decide whether to hold back the opening of financial bids while it replies. Check the tender document for how to send a representation.

## Caveats to keep in mind

Rules differ between departments and states. The tender document is final on what goes in each bid and on the opening dates. Many works tenders, especially small ones, use a single-envelope system, where eligibility, technical and price evaluation are done together. The Manual for Procurement of Works is a central Department of Expenditure document. CPWD, other central bodies and state departments have their own manuals, so check which one your tender follows.
